## Class Reviews
* **Overall**: Generally, this course might not be highly practical for students in my specific major or those in the IT-E10 program. It primarily focuses on optimization algorithms, which are predominantly applied to logistics, business operations, and similar domains. Students intending to specialize deeply in Machine Learning and this specialization or those pursuing advanced research on given fields are the ones who will likely find this course most beneficial.
* **Description**: The grading system follows a 40/60 scheme. Specifically, 40% of your overall grade evaluates your in-class progress throughout the semester (including your midterm project score and bonus points for diligence/participation), while the remaining 60% is determined by the final exam. The course spans approximately 15 weeks: the first 14 weeks are strictly dedicated to lectures, and the final week is reserved for project presentations. Please note that this specific syllabus is exclusively tailored for students in the IT-E15 (Cyber Security) and IT-E10 (AI & Data Science) programs. There is a counterpart course, MI3052, offered to other majors, but that version leans much more heavily into the theoretical mathematics of optimization. Due to this highly specialized curriculum, you might experience a scarcity of direct reference materials and online resources while studying for this exact course.
* **Instructor**: Mr. Trung is quite laid-back; he doesn't take attendance or strictly monitor your in-class focus. The base of your midterm grade is primarily determined by your performance in the mini-project presentations. Groups are formed randomly based on the class roster, but you have the freedom to choose your own topic. Keep in mind that you only have 15-20 minutes to present, so make sure your slides concisely summarize the core ideas and highlight the most critical features of your project. Additionally, make an effort to raise your hand and actively participate in class. He highly values this, and such engagement serves as a direct basis for earning bonus points toward your overall midterm grade. A word of caution: avoid relying heavily on AI-generated content, as he does not appreciate it and it will negatively impact your evaluation.
* **Final Exam**: The final exam consists of two parts, each accounting for 50% of the total score:
  * Part 1 (Theory Quiz): A multiple-choice quiz of about 22 questions. It focuses heavily on the Simplex method (e.g., understanding the components of a Simplex Tableau and calculating the next iteration). It also tests your knowledge of specific algorithms, such as Gomory's cut in the Constraint Satisfaction Problem (CSP) section, determining the next state in Greedy Hill Climbing, as well as the general properties and applications of these algorithms.
  * Part 2 (Coding Test): A practical coding test featuring three problems with difficulty levels scaled as Medium, Medium, and Hard, worth 5, 4, and 1 point(s) respectively. For the first two problems, you can typically use the Google OR-Tools library combined with fundamental knowledge of exact methods like Linear Programming (LP), Integer Linear Programming (ILP), and CSP. The third problem usually involves Heuristic algorithms, though aiming for a 9/10 overall is already an excellent goal.
  * During my semester, we were allowed to bring a single A4 cheat sheet into the exam room. I highly recommend using this sheet to meticulously note down the core mathematical principles of the algorithms to assist you with the quiz portion.
  * Please note that Jupyter Notebook is the only permitted code editor during the test. I highly recommend familiarizing yourself with this environment beforehand, especially how to handle standard inputs/outputs. Otherwise, you risk wasting valuable exam time just struggling with the interface inside the exam room.
